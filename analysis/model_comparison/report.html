The method uses a dual ascent learning approach with differentiable cross-entropy-based proxy constraints for fairness metrics (predictive equality, equal opportunity, demographic parity). Implemented as a LightGBM fork, FairGBM achieves an order of magnitude speedup compared to existing constrained optimization methods like Fairlearn EG, while maintaining or improving fairness-performance trade-offs on five benchmark datasets and a real-world account opening fraud case study. 83 words (target ~100) FairGBM is an in-processing fairness method for gradient boosted trees, implemented as a fork of LightGBM. Summary This paper formalizes the legal concept of 'Less Discriminatory Algorithm' (LDA) search as an optimal stopping problem. The authors develop an adaptive stopping algorithm with statistical guarantees that allows firms to certify when further model retraining is unlikely to yield meaningful disparate impact reductions. The approach decomposes the marginal gain into improvement probability and conditional expected improvement, providing high-probability upper bounds under various distributional assumptions. Empirical validation on credit and housing datasets shows the algorithm typically halts after modest retraining (tens of models), supporting the feasibility of demonstrating good-faith LDA search efforts. 92 words (target ~100) The paper frames Less Discriminatory Algorithm (LDA) search under model multiplicity as an optimal stopping problem. It gives an adaptive stopping algorithm, built on anytime-valid inference, that yields a high-probability upper bound on the disparate-impact gain from training more models, so a developer can certify that further retraining is unlikely to help. Optional distributional assumptions give tighter bounds, and a data-driven variant estimates the conditional expected improvement. Experiments use Adult, Folktables and HMDA with logistic regression, random forests and gradient boosting, plus Fairlearn. They show that disparate impact varies far more than accuracy across retrained models, and that a few dozen models usually suffice. 104 words (target ~100) Formalizes Less Discriminatory Alternative (LDA) search under model multiplicity as an optimal stopping problem. A developer repeatedly retrains models (random seeds, splits, hyperparameters, conditioned on meeting an accuracy threshold) and wants to certify when further search is no longer worth the cost. The marginal gain is decomposed into an improvement probability and a conditional expected improvement. The paper derives anytime-valid high-probability bounds on each, giving Algorithm 1, plus Algorithm 2, which estimates the conditional-improvement term from data. Tighter bounds are available under tail assumptions on the disparate-impact distribution. Experiments on Adult, Folktables and HMDA with logistic regression, random forests, GBTs and Fairlearn variants show the stopping rule overshoots the oracle stopping time by tens of models. 116 words (target ~100) The authors formalize the search for less discriminatory algorithms (LDAs) under model multiplicity as an optimal stopping problem: a firm repeatedly retrains with different random seeds/splits, keeps the model with the lowest test-set selection-rate disparity, and needs to know when further retraining is unlikely to pay off. Algorithm 1 combines a universal bound on conditional expected improvement with a novel anytime-valid bound on the probability of beating the running minimum (Lemma 3.2), yielding a high-probability certificate that the expected marginal disparate-impact gain from one more model is below a cost threshold γ.
